## What is wrong with the current calibration

Relevant code:

- `apps/desktop/src/renderer/WebGazerCalibration.tsx`
- `apps/desktop/src/main/webgazer-host.ts`
- `apps/desktop/src/main/gaze-overlay.ts`
- `apps/desktop/src/main/gaze-motion.ts`
- `apps/desktop/src/main/desktop-experience.ts` (uses gaze for desktop context)
- `apps/desktop/src/main/index.ts` (constructs host, permissions, protocol)

Current renderer flow is **our custom orchestration**, not WebGazer's official demo workflow:

1. Nine timed training targets, five automatic `recordScreenPosition(...,'click')` calls each, after one-second settling and at 250ms intervals.
2. Five additional unseen targets. Collect predictions for roughly 1.3 seconds after settling, requiring at least eight predictions.
3. Compute per-axis RMS errors, including bias and jitter, and send five error pairs to main.
4. Main rejects RMS Euclidean error above `0.16 * min(display.width, display.height)` or any target error above 1.6 times that limit.
5. Renderer hides camera/face feedback (`showVideoPreview(false)` and feedback flags false), disables saving across sessions, and calls `removeMouseEventListeners()` after startup. After training there is no ongoing calibration input.

**User report on October 14:** repeated failed attempts; message exactly:

> Calibration error is 175 points. Try again with steady lighting and your head comfortably still.

That message and rejection cutoff are ours. “Points” are screen-coordinate logical/CSS points. The metric is RMS distance derived from our test targets, not degrees or a vendor error. Do not compare 175 points on a Retina display directly with 175 pixels in an old paper.

Confirmed shortcomings:

- Automatic timing lacks user-paced confirmation that they are looking at each target.
- Camera/face feedback was hidden.
- The hard cutoff was not validated with real users.
- WebGazer's continual self-calibration from click/cursor interactions was disabled. Its default listeners only see the renderer's DOM, so simply re-enabling them in a hidden window does **not** provide desktop-wide adaptation.
- Current eye-patch feature mapping does not explicitly use head-pose geometry. MediaPipe finding a face is not equivalent to gaze compensation for head movement.
- Physical lid tilt, camera tilt and changed sitting angle are not detected by macOS display-arrangement checks. A model can continue producing plausible but wrong coordinates after these changes.
- Upstream `getPositions()` can be stale when no face is currently detected; inspect freshness and actual accepted sample behavior rather than assuming every non-null landmark array proves a usable training observation.

Previous assistant acknowledged that choosing the module plus a custom calibration wrapper did not fulfill the user's request faithfully. Do not defend the old acceptance test count as proof that gaze is usable.

## Research completed October 14

### WebGazer official calibration and limits

Primary sources read:

- https://webgazer.cs.brown.edu/
- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/brownhci/WebGazer/master/www/js/calibration.js
- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/brownhci/WebGazer/master/www/js/main.js
- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/brownhci/WebGazer/master/www/js/precision_calculation.js
- https://jeffhuang.com/papers/WebGazer_IJCAI16.pdf

Official demo uses five **user clicks** at each of nine points, center point last; visible camera feedback; a five-second center-fixation measurement with a displayed percentage and a recalibrate/continue choice. Its center-only metric is not a rigorous guarantee of accuracy elsewhere on screen; do not blindly label it a validated desktop acceptance threshold.

WebGazer's original paper describes continual self-calibration from clicks/cursor activity as its way of adapting to changes in the eye/head mapping, rather than explicit 3D reasoning. Original paper reports around 175/210 pixel errors for two models under those study conditions. The current project website explicitly says today's implementation no longer corresponds to the implementations in those papers. Peer review supports the research, not a blanket promise about the installed npm version or user's Mac.

Independent WebGazer validation previously read:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11289066/
It supports coarse areas of interest and documents precision/noise limitations; page access later hit a captcha. Do not use that to claim precise desktop controls or unrestricted posture changes are proven.
